**The close rate debate between sales and marketing usually ends the same way: nobody agrees on the benchmark. Here's the number I use after 8 years of mediating this fight.**

I've been in hundreds of sales-versus-marketing standoffs. Agency says the leads are great. Sales says the leads are garbage. The business owner is stuck in the middle with no framework for figuring out who's right.

A reliable close rate benchmark doesn't solve everything, but it gives you somewhere to start. Here's how I think about it.

## What Is Considered a Good Close Rate?

**For most service businesses generating leads through Google Ads or Meta Ads, a 20% close rate is the directional benchmark.**

Closing 20% or more of qualified leads means sales is doing its job. Under 20% is a signal to look more closely at your sales process before blaming the leads.

This isn't a perfect number. Close rates vary significantly by:
- Price point (higher ticket = lower close rate, generally)
- Industry (home services close higher than B2B SaaS)
- Lead source (referrals close higher than cold ad leads)
- Sales cycle length (same-day decisions vs. 90-day enterprise cycles)

Use 20% as a directional filter, not a law.

## How to Use Close Rate to Diagnose Problems

This is the actual value of the benchmark — not bragging about hitting it, but using it to figure out where in the funnel you're leaking.

**If close rate is under 20%:**
Start with the sales process before the leads. Are calls being answered? Is follow-up happening fast enough? Are salespeople handling objections well? Is the offer clearly communicated?

Only after ruling out sales process issues should you look at lead quality as the culprit.

**If close rate is above 20% but volume is too low:**
This is a marketing problem — not enough leads coming in, or CPL is too high to generate enough volume at your budget. Sales is fine. The top of the funnel needs work.

**If close rate is above 20% and you're still not profitable:**
Go back to customer acquisition cost. If your CAC is above one-third of LTV even with a good close rate, your prices are too low. The math doesn't work regardless of how well sales is performing.

## The Number Nobody Tracks: Qualified Close Rate vs. Total Close Rate

There's an important distinction that most businesses miss.

**Total close rate** counts all leads — including ones that were never qualified in the first place. Someone submitting a form out of curiosity, a competitor doing research, wrong geography, wrong budget. If those leads are in your denominator, your close rate looks terrible for the wrong reason.

**Qualified close rate** only counts leads that met your basic criteria for being a real prospect.

Always measure close rate against qualified leads. Otherwise you're measuring the combined performance of marketing targeting AND sales execution — and you can't separate which one is the problem.

## When to Stop Optimizing Close Rate

The goal isn't a perfect close rate. It's beating average.

If your industry average is 20% and you're at 28%, you're above average. Stop spending energy on close rate optimization and focus on volume, lead quality, or LTV instead. Chasing perfection on a metric that's already working is the wrong use of resources.

Know the benchmark. Beat it. Move on to the next constraint.
